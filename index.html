Revisiting the Fundamentals of Swarm Robotics: Event-based Analysis of Biological Swarms

Abstract

Scalability and highly dynamic coordination remain primary challenges in aerial swarm robotics, despite recent advances in the agility of individual autonomous aerial robots. To tackle these challenges, we revisit some of the core methods and assumptions commonly applied in swarm robotics by studying the underlying principles of biological systems that inspire and motivate most of the research in this field. Specifically, we analyze behavior of large-scale flocks of Columba livia (rock dove) using data captured by a pair of event cameras. This setup provides novel insights into existing research that relies on standard cameras or GNSS trackers; namely, it allows for microsecond-accurate temporal resolution of fully reconstructed 3D trajectories together with wing motion and other visual cues that may be utilized by the animals for agile coordination. Our analysis suggests that observed biological flocks utilize a dynamic hierarchy mechanism, which challenges consensus-based approaches commonly applied in robotics. As we demonstrate by experiments simulating fixed-wing UAVs, a swarming rule based on dynamic hierarchy not only better explains the observed data, but also matches the baselines' efficiency with more consistent performance. We publish details about our methodology including the design of the event stereo camera device for capturing the data, open-source implementation, as well as the datasets, to stimulate further research of biological swarms and the transfer of these findings to robotic systems.
